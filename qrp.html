Overview

QRP radio transmission is a communication protocal within the amateur radio community involving maximum transmission range and informational content using minimal power. This radio design philosophy is based on the U.S. FCC Part 97 rule mandating that amateurs should “use the minimum power necessary to carry out the desired communications.”

Texting via QRP involves using PSK31 digital encoding of text information within an amateur radio frequency band. To transmit a message, PSK31 is used to convert binary inputs to an analog waveform and the computers sound card is used to create an audio wave train of the binary sequence by imparting to an audio tone where 180◦ phase shift represents a 0 and no phase shift represents a 1. A typical radio frequency (RF) transmission band used for this purpose is the high frequency (3-30MHz or ITU band 7), specifically the 40-meter wavelength, or 7.1MHz. We were tasked with designing an amplifier for a QRP Texting Transmission circuit involving a NXP SA612 and 75Ω antenna.

The goal of the design project is to meet the criteria defined in below. The expected input signal for the amplifier originates from the SA612A mixer/oscillator integrated circuit, which will deliver a modulated 7.1 MHz signal to our amplifier that contains audio wave from the PSK31 converted signal. The SA612A has an output impedance of 1.5 kΩ, which is responsible for the design specification of the amplifier input resistance being equal to 1.5 kΩ ± 20% over the entire bandwidth of operation. The output of the amplifier will drive a 75 Ω coaxial cable to an antenna with minimal power loss and reflections. This is maintained by the specification of a strict output impedance of 75 Ω ± 20% over the entire bandwidth. To allow for long distance transmission, the gain specification goal is 60dB of voltage gain within ± 1.5dB over the full bandwidth of at 500kHz – 7.2MHz. The maximum allocated dc current target goal is 12mA or less from a voltage supply of +9V.
